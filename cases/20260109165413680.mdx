![Webasto — original case cover](/images/cases/20260109165413680/cover.webp)

Webasto supplies automotive roof and thermal systems worldwide. As part of its Asia-Pacific integration strategy, the company consolidated previously separate operations in Japan, South Korea, India, and Thailand into a shared services center in Shanghai.

For the accounts payable team, this meant standardizing invoice processing across the region and ensuring that extracted information could flow directly into SAP.

## One shared center, three invoice environments

The finance team needed to handle Chinese VAT invoices alongside commercial invoices from Japanese and South Korean suppliers. Although they served the same business purpose, the documents differed significantly in language, layout, and format.

Webasto's existing system struggled with Japanese and Korean text, particularly handwritten entries and non-standard invoice layouts. Staff had to rely on language specialists to enter and verify information manually, adding time and cost to the process.

As more regional operations moved into Shanghai, the workload increased. Month-end and quarter-end closing periods brought additional pressure, and the team needed to absorb the new volume without expanding headcount at the same pace. Chinese invoice processing also had to maintain reliable tax compliance checks.

## Bringing multilingual invoices into one workflow

Building on an earlier invoice automation project in China, Webasto extended its partnership with INTSIG DocFlow to support the wider APAC operation.

DocFlow's multilingual OCR engine is optimized for Japanese and Korean fonts, layouts, and handwriting conventions. It extracts structured information from supplier invoices, including handwritten entries and complex remarks fields, reducing the need for manual language-specific data entry and verification.

![A Korean invoice and extracted fields from the original customer case](/images/cases/20260109165413680/figure-1.webp)

Processing capacity was another part of the requirement. DocFlow's concurrent processing architecture handles dozens of overseas invoices on a typical day and can accommodate peak volumes of approximately 100 invoices per day, including during closing periods.

The integration also connects DocFlow with Webasto's existing Tangro and SAP systems. Invoice capture, multilingual data extraction, Chinese tax compliance checks, voucher data generation, and submission to SAP are linked in one accounts payable workflow.

![A Chinese VAT invoice and its extraction results in the original interface](/images/cases/20260109165413680/figure-2.webp)

## Five minutes became 15 seconds

The difference is most visible in everyday invoice processing. The average time required to process a single supplier invoice, including complex remarks and handwritten content, fell from approximately five minutes to just 15 seconds, a reduction of about 95%.

Japanese and South Korean invoices were brought into the automated workflow without the need to hire additional language-specific data-entry staff. The system now handles dozens of overseas invoices daily, with peak volumes reaching approximately 100 per day.

For Chinese invoices, the tax compliance check pass rate has remained above 99%, supporting a more standardized and reliable financial process.

By connecting multilingual document recognition with its existing financial systems, Webasto has made regional invoice processing less dependent on manual intervention. The Shanghai shared services center can now support a broader APAC operation with greater processing capacity and a more consistent workflow.
