![Heavy equipment manufacturer — original case cover](/images/cases/20260515161812188/cover.webp)

The company is a major construction machinery manufacturer with extensive international operations. As it advances its digital transformation, the company is bringing AI and data technologies into research, manufacturing, commercial operations, and customer services.

But as these initiatives expanded, different departments encountered a common obstacle: critical business information was locked inside documents that conventional tools struggled to process.

## The same document problem showed up across the business

In engineering, drawings contain borders, lines, and other graphical elements that conventional recognition tools can mistake for tables. This can affect the accuracy of extracted information used in downstream cost analysis.

Overseas finance and service teams face a different set of documents. International invoices vary widely in format, while repair manuals contain dense tables, images, and multi-level sections. The information needs to remain searchable and traceable so that employees can find the right details when processing invoices or supporting equipment maintenance.

Contract review brings its own difficulties. Legal and commercial teams need to check seals and signatures across multi-page, non-standard overseas contracts, including text partially obscured by stamps.

Internal audit teams, meanwhile, work with management reports submitted by different business units in inconsistent formats. Manually interpreting these documents takes time and can make important risks harder to identify.

The same issue also affects knowledge management. Technical documents containing formulas, multiple columns, and complex layouts often lose their original structure during conventional parsing, making it difficult to build reliable enterprise knowledge bases.

![An engineering drawing from the original case](/images/cases/20260515161812188/figure-1.webp)

## One parsing engine for very different documents

The group brought in TextIn xParse as a shared parsing engine for unstructured documents across its business systems. Instead of building separate document processing pipelines for individual departments, the company could use a common parsing capability to support different workflows.

xParse processes engineering drawings, overseas invoices, and technical manuals while preserving headings, paragraphs, and document hierarchy. It also identifies and labels key elements such as text and images, allowing downstream applications to select relevant content and create structured knowledge chunks.

![The engineering drawing and its structured parsing output](/images/cases/20260515161812188/figure-2.webp)

Repair manuals presented a more specific challenge. Their tables often extend across multiple pages, and extracting individual values is not enough if the relationships between rows, columns, and headers are lost.

xParse reconstructs these table structures across page boundaries, keeping cell values associated with the correct headers. Service applications can then use the structured output to retrieve equipment fault codes and relevant repair instructions.

![A dense multi-page technical table and the original extraction interface](/images/cases/20260515161812188/figure-3.webp)

For contract review, xParse provides seal detection and recognition of text obscured by stamps. It can identify seals across multi-page documents and extract underlying text, helping legal and commercial teams examine important contractual information more thoroughly.

The same parsing service also connects with the group's existing business systems and workflow platforms through standard APIs and private deployment, including Dify. Its structured outputs provide the foundation for downstream large language model (LLM) information extraction, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) knowledge bases, and internal AI assistants. The business applications continue to own their respective review logic and decisions.

## Where the work started to move faster

Once structured document data became available to downstream applications, the impact extended beyond document recognition.

In overseas procurement and finance, invoice approval time fell from 2 minutes to 1 minute per invoice, while the reported error rate dropped from 1.2% to 0.2%. Based on an annual volume of 40,000 invoices, the group estimated that the improvement could save approximately 80 person-days per year. It also projected an annual reduction of approximately ¥‌600,000 in risk costs associated with duplicate reimbursements.

For overseas maintenance teams, system retrieval time fell from 13 seconds to 5 seconds, a reduction of more than 60%. The broader process of locating equipment fault codes and repair instructions also moved from hours to minutes, helping engineers access relevant technical information more quickly.

The group's global marketing platform uses structured document information to support frontline sales and cross-department collaboration.

- **70% improvement** in frontline information retrieval efficiency
- **50% improvement** in cross-department collaboration efficiency
- **60% improvement** in proposal preparation efficiency
- **More than 40%** share of high-quality content generated with AI
- **43% improvement** in per-person productivity

The implementation also helped teams resolve customer questions more quickly and improve customer satisfaction.

Internal audit saw a similar change. In the group's audit and early-warning center, management report analysis efficiency improved by 70%. The average time required for an audit task fell from four to five hours to less than 30 minutes, while the identification rate for key risk points reached 98%.

Across these workflows, xParse has become more than a document recognition tool. It provides a shared data layer that allows different departments to reuse the same parsing capabilities and connect complex documents with the systems and AI applications that depend on them.

For the group, this creates a more scalable foundation for enterprise AI adoption, overseas operations, knowledge management, and risk control.
